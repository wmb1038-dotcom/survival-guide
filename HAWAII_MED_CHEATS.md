# Hawaii Emergency Medical Cheat Sheets
*Adapted from ST-31-91B Special Forces Medical Handbook for Tropical Environments*

---

## 1. LEPTOSPIROSIS (Stream Water/Mud)
Hawaii’s most common waterborne bacterial threat. Enters through cuts or mucous membranes after contact with freshwater contaminated by animal urine (pigs/rats).

**Prevention Protocols:**
* **Water Safety:** Assume all inland freshwater (streams, waterfalls, taro patches) is contaminated. Use 0.1-micron filtration OR rolling boil.
* **Wound Protection:** Cover any open cuts with waterproof bandages before entering mud/water. 
* **PPE:** Wear tabis/boots in muddy terrain; avoid submerging head in stagnant pools.

**Symptom Tracking (Days 2-14):**
* Sudden high fever, chills, and severe "back-of-the-eye" headache.
* **Key Indicator:** Calf muscle tenderness (myalgia) and conjunctival suffusion (red eyes without discharge).

**Austere Care:**
* **Hydration:** Strict oral rehydration protocol.
* **Antibiotics:** Doxycycline (100mg 2x daily for 7 days) is the SF standard for early treatment/prophylaxis.

**🚩 RED FLAGS (Evacuate Immediately):**
* Jaundice (yellowing eyes/skin) indicating liver failure (Weil’s Disease).
* Dark/bloody urine or reduced output (kidney failure).
* Difficulty breathing or coughing up blood.

## 2. TROPICAL STAPH & CORAL CUTS
High humidity and heat accelerate bacterial growth. "Coral cuts" are often polymicobial (Vibrio + Staph) and can turn septic within 24 hours.

**Immediate Field Care:**
* **Scrub:** Immediately scrub the cut with soap and fresh water. Use a soft brush to remove coral polyps/calcium carbonate.
* **Disinfect:** Flush with 3% Hydrogen Peroxide or Betadine. 
* **Dry:** Keep the wound dry. In 80%+ humidity, "airing out" often fails; use a light breathable dressing and change 3x daily.

**Tropical Infection Protocol:**
* **Marking:** Draw a line around the redness with a pen to track spreading (cellulitis).
* **Heat:** Apply warm, moist compresses for 20 mins 4x daily to draw out infection.

**🚩 RED FLAGS (Surgical/Systemic Logic):**
* **Lymphangitis:** Red streaks moving up the limb toward the heart.
* **Fluctuance:** A soft, "squishy" center in the redness indicating an abscess that requires I&D (Incision and Drainage).
* **Crepitus:** A "crunchy" feeling under the skin (gas gangrene/necrotizing fasciitis).

## 3. HEAT STROKE (Leeward/Kapolei Logic)
Oahu’s Leeward coast creates a "Heat Island" effect. High humidity prevents evaporative cooling (sweat doesn't dry).

**Field Cooling Logic:**
* **Strip:** Remove all clothing. 
* **Wet & Wind:** Spray with water and use a battery fan or manual waving. *Evaporation is 10x more effective than still water.*
* **Ice Points:** Apply cold packs/wet rags to the "Great Vessels": Groin, Axilla (armpits), and Neck.

**Hydration Logic:**
* **The "Clear" Rule:** Monitor urine; if it’s darker than apple juice, you are in the danger zone.
* **Salt Balance:** In tropical heat, drinking 2+ gallons of plain water can cause Hyponatremia (salt depletion). Mix 1 tsp salt + 6 tsp sugar per liter of water.

**🚩 RED FLAGS (Heat Stroke vs. Exhaustion):**
* **Mental Status:** Confusion, combativeness, or loss of consciousness.
* **Anhidrosis:** The patient stops sweating despite the heat (indicates core thermoregulation failure).
* **Seizures:** Immediate neurological emergency.

## 4. CIGUATERA (Fish Poisoning)
A heat-stable toxin found in reef fish (Ulua, Roi, Kole) that have eaten toxic algae. *Cooking does NOT destroy the toxin.*

**Identification & Prevention:**
* **Predators:** Avoid large apex reef predators (Barracuda, Large Ulua).
* **Parts:** Never eat the head, liver, or roe (toxin concentration is 50x higher).

**Symptom Recognition:**
* **Temperature Reversal:** Cold things feel hot, hot things feel cold (pathognomonic sign).
* **Neurological:** Tingling in lips/fingertips, joint pain, and profound weakness.

**Care:**
* **Activated Charcoal:** If ingested within 2-4 hours.
* **Mannitol:** Standard clinical treatment, but in field settings, focus on managing dehydration and preventing aspiration if vomiting.

**🚩 RED FLAGS:**
* Bradycardia (heart rate <50 bpm).
* Hypotension (fainting) or respiratory paralysis.

## 5. RAT LUNGWORM (Angiostrongylus)
Parasite carried by rats and spread by slugs/snails. Extremely prevalent in East Oahu/Puna, but exists statewide.

**Contamination Protocols:**
* **Slug Check:** Slugs can be microscopic or hide in the "furl" of Manoa lettuce.
* **Wash Logic:** Do not just rinse. Submerge greens in a 5% vinegar solution or salt water for 10 mins. This causes slugs to detach.
* **Heat:** Parasites die at 145°F (63°C). Cook all local greens in high-risk zones.

**Symptom Warning:**
* Eosinophilic meningitis: Severe "electric shock" pains, neck stiffness, and skin sensitivity.

**🚩 RED FLAGS:**
* Inability to touch chin to chest (Meningismus).
* Severe photophobia (light hurts eyes).
* Coma or altered mental state.
